# Behavioral Feedback for Adjustment

## Operational definition

Behavioral feedback is information about observed action or outcome interpreted against a defined reference and used to decide whether to maintain, investigate or adjust a plan.

## What this concept does not mean

A score is not automatically useful feedback. Feedback is not praise, blame, surveillance or a causal explanation. More frequent reporting is not always better.

## Main mechanisms

Monitoring creates a record; feedback connects that record to a goal or expectation. The discrepancy, stability and cost of performance can inform whether the next decision is to preserve the current mechanism, change one variable or gather more information.

## Evidence summary

A meta-analysis of 138 experimental studies found that interventions increasing progress monitoring improved goal attainment on average, with larger effects in some reporting and recording conditions. A meta-review of health behavior found personalized feedback among sometimes successful self-regulation components, but no component worked consistently across behaviors and populations. Feedback should therefore remain specific and actionable without being treated as universally effective.

## Evidence mapping

- Increasing progress monitoring promoted goal attainment on average: `src-progress-monitoring-2016` (`meta_analysis_of_experiments`).
- Feedback and other self-regulation components had heterogeneous effects: `src-self-regulation-2020` (`systematic_meta_review`).

## Relevant signals

Records exist but are used only for judgment, stable progress triggers needless escalation, or repeated misses do not lead to a defined review.

## Alternative explanations

The measure may be invalid, opportunities incomparable, the reference inappropriate or the observation window too short.

## Information needed

Behavior and outcome measures, reference, observation window, comparable opportunities, performance cost and the decision the data can change.

## Practical implications

State the observation separately from interpretation. Maintain a working plan when progress and cost are stable; otherwise adjust one plausible variable for a defined test period.

## Limitations

Monitoring interventions often contain multiple components and may change behavior through reporting or accountability. Feedback can demotivate or mislead when measures are poor.
