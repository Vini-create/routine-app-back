# Observable Behavior

## Operational definition

An observable-behavior description replaces a global judgment about a person
with an account that another observer could recognize: the action or non-action,
its context, frequency or duration when relevant, and its near consequence. The
unit of analysis is an episode, not a personality trait.

## What this concept does not mean

It does not deny emotions, reduce a person to metrics or require exhaustive
tracking. A precise description also does not establish why the behavior
occurred or what function it served.

## Main mechanisms

Operational description makes the target and context explicit. This permits
different explanations—opportunity, skill, energy, clarity, access, safety or
priority—to remain distinguishable instead of being collapsed into a moral
label.

## Evidence summary

BCTO and BCTTv1 provide vocabularies for specifying observable, replicable
intervention content. They are descriptive frameworks. They are not causal
tests, diagnostic instruments or evidence that observing a behavior will by
itself change it.

## Evidence mapping

- BCTO defines behavior change techniques as observable, replicable intervention components: `src-bcto-2024` (`canonical_framework`).
- BCTTv1 standardizes descriptions of intervention content: `src-bcttv1-2013` (`canonical_framework`).

## Relevant signals

The available account consists mainly of labels such as lazy, weak,
undisciplined or incapable; an intention is reported without the moment of
execution; or competing explanations depend on different episodes.

## Alternative explanations

The same non-action may reflect insufficient time, knowledge, energy, access or
priority. It may also be a reasonable response to risk or changing conditions.

## Information needed

A recent episode, the intended action, what occurred instead, the context when
it changes interpretation, and the immediate consequence. Frequency is needed
only when the decision depends on a repeated pattern.

## Practical implications

Use the smallest episode description that can discriminate the relevant
alternatives. Missing records remain missing data; they are not evidence that a
failure occurred.

## Limitations

Reports may omit context or be affected by recall. Observability improves
description, not completeness, causality or clinical validity.
