# Decision-Oriented Self-Monitoring

## Operational definition

Self-monitoring is recording a defined behavior or outcome to support a later
decision. A usable record specifies the variable, collection frequency,
observation period and the decision that the information may change.

## What this concept does not mean

It is not continuous surveillance, a score of personal worth or evidence that
an unrecorded event failed to occur. More variables and longer collection do not
automatically improve inference.

## Main mechanisms

Consistent observation can make discrepancies, context and change visible.
Defining the decision first limits collection burden and reduces the temptation
to interpret every fluctuation after the fact.

## Evidence summary

BCTTv1 defines self-monitoring of behavior and outcomes as separate techniques.
A meta-review of self-regulation interventions found variable effectiveness
across domains and components. Monitoring is therefore a way to obtain decision
data, not a guarantee of behavior change.

## Evidence mapping

- BCTTv1 distinguishes self-monitoring of behavior from self-monitoring of outcomes: `src-bcttv1-2013` (`canonical_framework`).
- Self-regulation components showed variable effectiveness across reviewed behaviors and populations: `src-self-regulation-2020` (`systematic_review`).

## Relevant signals

A decision depends on a pattern that is not yet observable; conclusions are
being drawn from incomplete coverage; or collection burden exceeds the value of
the possible decision.

## Alternative explanations

A change in the record may reflect missing entries, changed opportunities,
measurement drift or ordinary fluctuation rather than a behavioral change.

## Information needed

The decision question, observable variable, collection method, expected
coverage, burden, privacy implications, observation window and handling of
missing data.

## Practical implications

Collect the minimum variable for the shortest window that can distinguish the
relevant alternatives. Report coverage and missingness with the result.

## Limitations

Self-report and automated logs can both be incomplete. Observational patterns
do not establish causality, and intrusive collection can create privacy or
behavioral costs.
