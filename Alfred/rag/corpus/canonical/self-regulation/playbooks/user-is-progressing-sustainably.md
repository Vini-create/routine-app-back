# User Is Making Sustainable Progress

## Activation criteria

The user reports repeated progress across comparable opportunities with acceptable cost and no urgent problem, and asks how to respond or whether to change the plan.

## Similar situations that should not activate this playbook

Use rapid-progression routing when the user proposes a large increase. Use tiredness or capacity routing when progress is purchased through accumulating fatigue, sleep loss, pain or neglected obligations.

## Possible explanations

The current plan may fit the context and capacity, a cue or schedule may be working, the task grade may be appropriate, or the observation window may be too short to establish stability. Reported success can also hide unmeasured cost.

## Missing information

The exact behavior, observation period, comparable opportunities, completion and quality, time and effort cost, relevant recovery or competing-goal cost, and the reason the user is considering change.

## Response objective

Recognize the observed success, identify the mechanism worth preserving and avoid unnecessary optimization. Treat maintenance as an active decision when evidence supports it.

## Decision path

- If behavior and cost are stable across a meaningful window, maintain the current version and review less frequently.
- If progress is stable but quality is not, adjust the quality criterion rather than increasing volume automatically.
- If success depends on a specific cue, support or schedule, preserve that condition where feasible.
- If the window is short or opportunities differ, gather more comparable observations before escalation.
- If hidden cost is rising, reclassify the situation as a capacity or progression problem.

## Candidate strategies

Maintain the plan, name and protect the working condition, reduce monitoring burden, set a maintenance review, or change one justified variable.

## Conditions for selecting each strategy

Reduced monitoring is appropriate only when it will not remove needed feedback. A change needs a specific objective, not boredom with a functioning routine.

## When to ask a question

Ask what the progress is costing in time, energy or displaced goals when sustainability has been asserted but not described.

## When to respond directly

Respond directly when behavior, duration and cost are clear and stable.

## What to avoid

Do not manufacture a problem, demand constant growth, praise identity rather than evidence, or equate an uninterrupted streak with sustainability.

## Suggested next step

Keep the current plan for a defined maintenance interval and record only the signal that would justify review.
