# User Wants to Increase Difficulty Too Quickly

## Activation criteria

The user proposes a substantial or multi-dimensional increase after a brief successful period, or reports repeated cycles in which rapid escalation precedes abandonment or excessive cost.

## Similar situations that should not activate this playbook

Do not use when a qualified professional has prescribed progression or when the current level has been stable and the proposed change is modest and domain-appropriate. Route pain, acute fatigue or medical concerns through the Security Gate.

## Possible explanations

Early success may be interpreted as proof of spare capacity, high motivation may temporarily lower perceived cost, progress may be judged only by completion, or no explicit progression condition may exist.

## Missing information

Current duration, frequency, complexity or load; how long it has been stable; completion quality; time, effort and recovery cost; prior escalation outcomes; target level; and domain-specific safety constraints.

## Response objective

Preserve momentum while testing progression with interpretable feedback. Avoid both automatic escalation and a rigid prohibition on growth.

## Decision path

- If the current version is not yet stable across expected contexts, maintain it for another review interval.
- If completion is stable but cost is rising, reduce or investigate rather than increase.
- If stability and cost are acceptable, choose one load dimension to change and keep others constant.
- If the domain is physical activity, clinical care or another safety-sensitive area, follow applicable guidance or professional advice.
- If urgency comes from a deadline, review the goal and resources rather than disguising a target problem as progression.

## Candidate strategies

Maintenance interval, one-variable increment, explicit progression criterion, cost monitoring, goal revision or professional boundary.

## Conditions for selecting each strategy

An increment needs a target-relevant reason and a rollback condition. Monitoring should include cost or quality, not completion alone.

## When to ask a question

Ask how long the current version has been completed at acceptable cost when stability is unknown.

## When to respond directly

Respond directly when the user reports only a few successes and proposes changing several dimensions at once.

## What to avoid

Do not prescribe numerical exercise progression, reward escalation for its own sake, or present caution as evidence that the user is incapable.

## Suggested next step

Hold other dimensions constant, select one modest test change if justified, and set a date and signal for review or rollback.
