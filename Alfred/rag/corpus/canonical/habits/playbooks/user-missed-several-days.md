# User Missed Several Planned Days

## Activation criteria

The user reports multiple missed intended opportunities for a nonclinical routine and wants to decide how to resume. At least one prior performance and a plausible next opportunity exist.

## Similar situations that should not activate this playbook

Do not use for medication, substance use, disordered eating, rehabilitation or another condition needing professional guidance. Route low energy, acute symptoms or severe distress through the Security Gate. Use cannot-start when the behavior has never begun.

## Possible explanations

The misses may be an ordinary short lapse, a changed cue or schedule, reduced capacity, loss of access, an overly difficult version, incomplete recording or a goal the user no longer endorses.

## Missing information

Which planned opportunities were genuine, what changed before the first miss, whether the same barrier repeated, current capacity, the next feasible opportunity and whether resuming is still wanted.

## Response objective

Separate interruption from global failure, identify whether conditions changed and choose a feasible next response without trying to repair a streak or repay a behavioral debt.

## Decision path

- If the context and capacity are substantially unchanged, resume the ordinary version at the next real opportunity.
- If capacity fell, select a lower but still representative grade and set a review condition.
- If the cue or schedule changed, create a plan around the new recurring opportunity.
- If several misses share no clear cause, record the next two or three opportunities before redesigning the whole routine.
- If the goal is no longer chosen, review it rather than forcing a restart.

## Candidate strategies

Simple resumption, temporary graded resumption, context replanning, minimal opportunity tracking, or goal review.

## Conditions for selecting each strategy

Use simple resumption only when the next opportunity resembles prior successful ones. Reduce the grade for demonstrated capacity change, not as an automatic ritual after every miss.

## When to ask a question

Ask what changed immediately before the first missed opportunity when that answer distinguishes resumption from replanning.

## When to respond directly

Respond directly when the interruption was bounded, the cause is clear and a safe next opportunity is already available.

## What to avoid

Do not claim that prior learning vanished, demand make-up volume, recommend punishment, or treat the consecutive-day count as the main outcome.

## Suggested next step

Choose the next feasible opportunity and perform the version supported by current conditions; review the pattern after renewed observations.
