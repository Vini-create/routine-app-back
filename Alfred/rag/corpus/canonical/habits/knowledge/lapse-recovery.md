# Lapses, Streaks and Behavioral Recovery

## Operational definition

A lapse is a missed intended response during one or more genuine opportunities. Recovery is the return to a feasible next response after checking whether the opportunity, context, capacity or goal changed.

## What this concept does not mean

A lapse is not proof that a habit has disappeared, that prior repetitions were wasted or that the person lacks discipline. A streak is a count of consecutive recorded performances, not a direct measure of automaticity, quality or long-term maintenance.

## Main mechanisms

Habit learning accumulates through repeated cue-response experience and follows variable trajectories. A missed opportunity changes the record but does not logically erase prior learning. A longer interruption may expose changed contexts or capacity and therefore requires reassessment rather than automatic compensation.

## Evidence summary

In Lally and colleagues' observational model, missing one opportunity did not materially affect the automaticity trajectory, although this result came from a small sample and should not be generalized to every interruption. A later systematic review found wide variation in formation time and methods. Together, the evidence supports avoiding all-or-nothing conclusions, not promising that any lapse is harmless.

## Evidence mapping

- A single missed opportunity did not materially affect modelled automaticity in the observed sample: `src-habit-lally-2010` (`observational_study`).
- Habit trajectories and estimates varied substantially across a limited literature: `src-habit-review-2024` (`systematic_review`).

## Relevant signals

Several planned opportunities were missed, the user treats a broken streak as total loss, or proposes a sharp workload increase to “catch up.”

## Alternative explanations

The schedule, health, resources, cue or chosen goal may have changed. The record may also be incomplete.

## Information needed

Which opportunities were real, what changed before the first miss, current capacity, the next available opportunity and whether the goal remains endorsed.

## Practical implications

Resume at the next feasible grade, redistribute only when constraints allow, and review the cause if misses cluster. Judge recovery by renewed behavior and cost, not by restoring a streak.

## Limitations

Direct evidence about everyday “streaks” is sparse. This concept should not be used for substance-use relapse, eating disorders, medication adherence or other clinical conditions.
