# Graded Task Progression

## Operational definition

Graded task progression starts with a version that is within demonstrated capacity and increases difficulty in planned steps toward the target behavior. A grade can change duration, frequency, complexity, resistance or required independence.

## What this concept does not mean

It is not indefinite reduction, permission to ignore a chosen goal, or a universal rule to make every task tiny. It does not prescribe exercise load, rehabilitation or treatment progression.

## Main mechanisms

A feasible grade creates an opportunity to practice the relevant response and collect feedback. Predetermined progression conditions reduce the chance that a temporary surge in confidence becomes an untested increase across several dimensions.

## Evidence summary

The BCT Taxonomy defines graded tasks as setting easy-to-perform tasks and increasing difficulty until the target behavior is performed. A systematic review and meta-analysis of physical-activity interventions for adults with overweight or obesity found graded tasks among positive moderators in some analyses. This is domain-limited, component-level evidence and does not establish an optimal progression rate for an individual.

## Evidence mapping

- Graded tasks are a distinct, operationally defined behavior-change technique: `src-bcttv1-2013` (`consensus_taxonomy`).
- Graded tasks moderated effects in a specific physical-activity intervention literature: `src-graded-pa-2021` (`systematic_review_meta_analysis`).

## Relevant signals

The current version is repeatedly not completed despite real opportunities, or the user wants to increase several load dimensions after only brief success.

## Alternative explanations

The task may lack instructions, materials or endorsement. Noncompletion may reflect scheduling rather than task difficulty.

## Information needed

Current dose, recent completion and cost, target dose, safety constraints, dimension to change and a review interval.

## Practical implications

Keep the target visible, choose one feasible grade and define what evidence would justify changing one dimension. For physical or clinical activity, defer to applicable professional guidance.

## Limitations

Technique labels do not prove isolated efficacy. Evidence varies by behavior and population, and easier tasks can be irrelevant if they do not preserve the target response.
