# User Starts a Task and Then Stops

## Activation criteria

The user can identify at least one genuine start but reports that continuation or return did not occur. The decision concerns the drop-off after initiation, not inability to perform the first action.

## Similar situations that should not activate this playbook

Use the cannot-start playbook when no task-producing action occurred. Use lapse recovery when a previously stable routine was interrupted, and capacity or tiredness routing when reduced energy is the stated barrier.

## Possible explanations

The initial version may require more time or effort than was tolerable. A recurring barrier may appear only after setup, early enthusiasm may have supported an unsustainable dose, feedback may be absent, or the task may become aversive during execution. The schedule or goal may also have changed.

## Missing information

The last point reached, how many starts occurred, the exact point of stopping, time and effort spent, what happened next, whether another opportunity occurred, and whether the goal remains chosen.

## Response objective

Locate the first repeatable break in the continuation chain and test one matching adjustment without converting an unfinished attempt into a claim about persistence or character.

## Decision path

- If the first version consistently exceeds available capacity, reduce one load dimension and define a progression condition.
- If the same material, transition or location blocks continuation, change that observed friction.
- If unpleasantness rises after a specific stage, name the aversive component and bound or redesign that stage.
- If the user cannot tell where stopping occurs, collect a minimal start, stop and context record before prescribing.
- If the goal is no longer endorsed, leave execution coaching and review the goal.

## Candidate strategies

A graded version, transition cue, component-specific friction change, brief continuation log, or deliberate goal review.

## Conditions for selecting each strategy

Select the smallest intervention supported by the latest episode. A grade must still contain the target response; an environmental change needs a repeated barrier; tracking needs a decision it can inform.

## When to ask a question

Ask one branch-changing question, usually: “At what exact point did the last attempt stop, and what happened immediately after?”

## When to respond directly

Respond directly when the drop-off, constraint and next real opportunity are already stated.

## What to avoid

Do not restart with a larger commitment, assume a motivation deficit, prescribe a generic habit stack, or imply that an uninterrupted streak is the objective.

## Suggested next step

Re-enter at the last feasible task-producing component and observe whether the identified drop-off changes over the next comparable opportunities.
