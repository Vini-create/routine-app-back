# Temporal Discounting in Procrastination

## Operational definition

Temporal discounting is the reduction in subjective value assigned to an outcome as its delay increases. In procrastination, immediate relief or an available reward may outweigh a valued but distant benefit at the decision point.

## What this concept does not mean

It does not mean that every delay is impulsive, irrational or caused by one brain system. Choosing an immediate need can be reasonable when constraints or uncertainty change.

## Main mechanisms

The cost of beginning is experienced now, while completion benefits and many consequences arrive later. As a deadline approaches, the delayed consequence becomes more immediate, which can change the relative value of acting.

## Evidence summary

Steel's meta-analytic review found that task delay and impulsiveness were consistent predictors within a temporal motivation account. A 2024 study measured work progress over a real semester task and found a positive correlation between delay discounting and procrastination using the full progress trajectory, but not using completion day or self-report alone. This measurement sensitivity and correlational design require caution.

## Evidence mapping

- Delay-related predictors were consistent with temporal motivation theory: `src-procrastination-steel-2007` (`meta_analytic_review`).
- Discount rate correlated with a trajectory-based measure of real-world procrastination: `src-temporal-discounting-2024` (`correlational_primary_study`).

## Relevant signals

Work accelerates only near deadlines, an immediately rewarding substitute repeatedly wins, or the user explicitly describes present relief as stronger than a distant outcome.

## Alternative explanations

The deadline may be artificial, the first action unclear, the task aversive, or the outcome insufficiently valued.

## Information needed

Timing of costs and benefits, actual deadline, substitute reward, progress trajectory, first action and whether delay occurs without distant outcomes.

## Practical implications

Make progress and consequences nearer and observable, reduce delay to useful feedback, or precommit a concrete opportunity. Do not assume reward engineering is necessary before the pattern is observed.

## Limitations

One recent real-world study cannot establish individual causation. Discounting measures and procrastination measures are not interchangeable.
