# Perfectionistic Concerns and Evaluative Avoidance

## Operational definition

Evaluative avoidance is postponement or withholding that occurs when producing or exposing work creates an anticipated evaluative threat. The relevant perfectionistic dimension is usually concern about mistakes, discrepancy or external evaluation, not high standards by themselves.

## What this concept does not mean

Perfectionism is not a unitary explanation for procrastination. Careful work, ambitious standards and revision are not avoidance when they remain proportionate and lead to delivery.

## Main mechanisms

Delaying exposure can temporarily reduce the possibility of receiving evidence that performance falls short. Repeated checking or revision can also function as a safer substitute for submitting, testing or requesting feedback.

## Evidence summary

A 2017 meta-analysis found a positive association between procrastination and perfectionistic concerns, but a negative association with perfectionistic strivings. Steel's earlier review found only weak relationships for some fear-of-failure and socially prescribed perfectionism constructs. The combined evidence argues for assessing the specific concern and behavior rather than labeling the user a perfectionist.

## Evidence mapping

- Perfectionistic concerns and strivings showed different average relationships with procrastination: `src-procrastination-perfectionism-2017` (`meta_analysis`).
- Fear-of-failure constructs were not uniformly strong predictors: `src-procrastination-steel-2007` (`meta_analytic_review`).

## Relevant signals

Work is repeatedly revised but not exposed, a safe preparation activity replaces an evaluable output, or the user names anticipated judgment or mistakes at the decision point.

## Alternative explanations

The quality criterion may genuinely be unclear, the output may carry material risk, prerequisite skill may be missing, or more time may be justified.

## Information needed

The required standard, current draft state, who evaluates it, consequences of error, last delivery attempt and what would count as sufficient evidence for completion.

## Practical implications

Define a fit-for-purpose completion criterion and the smallest real exposure that can produce useful feedback. Preserve necessary quality and safety checks.

## Limitations

Most evidence is correlational. Fear, standards and delay can influence one another, and this concept is not a clinical assessment of anxiety or personality.
