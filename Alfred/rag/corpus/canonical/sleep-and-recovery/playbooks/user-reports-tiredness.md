# User Reports Tiredness or Short Sleep

## Activation criteria

After the Security Gate returns a noncritical result, the user links routine
execution to tiredness, short sleep or a temporary reduction in energy.

## Similar situations that should not activate this playbook

Do not label every resistance or loss of interest as tiredness. Do not use this
playbook when the Security Gate has selected a critical or clinical boundary.

## Possible explanations

The pattern may involve reduced sleep opportunity, shift work, workload,
temporary disruption or another cause that RAG cannot determine.

## Missing information

Whether the report is one episode or a sustained pattern, typical sleep
duration, schedule changes, functional impact and which commitments are
negotiable. The Security Gate owns all critical screening information.

## Response objective

Protect recovery and adjust negotiable routine demands without diagnosing the
cause or turning a population sleep range into an individual prescription.

## Decision path

- If this is an isolated noncritical disruption, reschedule or reduce a negotiable demand.
- If schedule structure repeatedly limits sleep opportunity, examine capacity and fixed commitments.
- If a sustained pattern causes impairment without a critical signal, keep coaching limited and identify appropriate external assessment.
- If the user asks for a medical cause or treatment change, do not continue with routine optimization.

## Candidate strategies

Protecting sleep opportunity, rescheduling a negotiable task, capacity review
and a boundary toward appropriate assessment.

## Conditions for selecting each strategy

Use rescheduling for an isolated disruption, capacity review for repeated
schedule conflict and an external-assessment boundary when the pattern persists
or the requested decision exceeds routine planning.

## When to ask a question

Ask only for noncritical information that changes the planning branch, such as
whether the pattern is isolated or recurrent and what can be moved.

## When to respond directly

Respond directly when the noncritical schedule conflict and negotiable demand
are already clear.

## What to avoid

Do not diagnose, recommend medication or supplements, prescribe an individual
sleep duration or optimize productivity by reducing sleep.

## Suggested next step

Protect the next realistic sleep opportunity or move one negotiable demand; if
the issue exceeds routine planning, stop at the appropriate boundary.
