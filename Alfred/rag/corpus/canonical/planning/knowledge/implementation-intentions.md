# Implementation Intentions

## Operational definition

An implementation intention links a discriminable situation to an available
response: if situation Y occurs, then action X will follow. It can support
starting, protecting or resuming action when a recurring obstacle appears.

## What this concept does not mean

It is not an ordinary schedule, positive thinking or an exhaustive list of
exceptions. It presupposes an endorsed goal and a response that is feasible in
the specified situation.

## Main mechanisms

The conditional link makes a relevant cue easier to recognize and preselects a
response before the obstacle occurs. This addresses execution without assuming
that strengthening intention alone will produce equivalent behavioral change.

## Evidence summary

Gollwitzer and Sheeran synthesized 94 tests and reported a positive average
effect of implementation intentions on goal attainment. Webb and Sheeran found
that experimentally changed intentions produced smaller changes in behavior,
supporting a distinction between wanting and executing. Effects vary by goal,
context and implementation quality.

## Evidence mapping

- If-then plans specifying when, where or how to act showed a positive aggregate effect on goal attainment: `src-ii-2006` (`meta_analysis`).
- Changes in intention were not fully translated into behavioral changes: `src-intention-behavior-2006` (`meta_analysis`).

## Relevant signals

The same recognizable event repeatedly precedes failure to start, interruption
or failure to resume, such as a delayed meeting, a specific distraction or a
return from travel.

## Alternative explanations

A rare, vague or uncontrollable obstacle may require capacity reserve,
negotiation or revision of the goal rather than a conditional response.

## Information needed

The endorsed goal, observable trigger, trigger frequency, control over the
response, response cost and any conflict with safety or fixed obligations.

## Practical implications

Prefer one high-value contingency over many hypothetical rules. Evaluate the
plan only after the trigger actually occurs; absence of the trigger is not
failure.

## Limitations

Aggregate effects do not guarantee individual benefit. Rigid contingencies can
be ineffective when opportunities and constraints change continuously.
