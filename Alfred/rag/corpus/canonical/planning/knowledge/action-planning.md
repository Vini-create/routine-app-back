# Executable Action Planning

## Operational definition

An action plan specifies a recognizable behavior, an opportunity or context,
a starting condition and enough scope to identify completion. It turns an
intention into an action that can be attempted under real constraints.

## What this concept does not mean

It is not a complete wish list, a schedule with no margin or a contingency plan
for every obstacle. Specificity cannot compensate for missing time, skill,
materials, access or authority.

## Main mechanisms

Specifying the behavior and opportunity reduces ambiguity between intending and
starting. A duration or scope boundary limits planning from expanding into an
unbounded task. Feasibility checks expose conflicts before execution.

## Evidence summary

BCTTv1 defines action planning as detailed planning of behavioral performance,
including at least one contextual element. A meta-review of self-regulation
interventions found variable support for planning and related components across
behaviors and populations; the component is not uniformly effective.

## Evidence mapping

- Action planning includes detailed planning of context and behavioral performance: `src-bcttv1-2013` (`canonical_framework`).
- Self-regulation components did not show consistent effectiveness across all reviewed domains: `src-self-regulation-2020` (`systematic_review`).

## Relevant signals

A clear intention is paired with “when I can”; the task has no recognizable
first action; a calendar block conflicts with existing commitments; or planned
scope is larger than the available opportunity.

## Alternative explanations

Non-execution may reflect insufficient ability, strong aversiveness, an imposed
goal, environmental barriers or inadequate capacity. Adding detail does not
resolve those conditions.

## Information needed

The target behavior, minimum useful scope, real opportunity, fixed commitments,
materials, dependencies, travel and available margin.

## Practical implications

Use action planning when intention and control are present but execution is
underspecified. Prefer a contingency concept when a recurring obstacle, rather
than the normal opportunity, determines the response.

## Limitations

Detailed plans can fail in unpredictable routines and can themselves become
additional work. Precision should remain proportional to the decision.
