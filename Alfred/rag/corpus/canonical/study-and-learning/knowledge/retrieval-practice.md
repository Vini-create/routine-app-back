# Retrieval Practice with Feedback

## Operational definition

Retrieval practice requires attempting to produce an answer from memory before
consulting the solution, followed by feedback or correction. The target is
retention and later use, not familiarity while looking at the material.

## What this concept does not mean

It is not merely rereading a question and answer, copying notes or taking a
high-stakes test. Repeated retrieval without corrective feedback can preserve
errors, and retrieval cannot replace initial comprehension.

## Main mechanisms

Effortful access strengthens later accessibility and reveals what cannot yet be
produced. Feedback corrects errors and distinguishes a retrieval problem from a
gap in prior understanding.

## Evidence summary

Dunlosky and colleagues rated practice testing as a high-utility learning
technique across conditions. A later classroom meta-analysis found a positive
average effect of retrieval practice, with variation by design, feedback,
material and educational context.

## Evidence mapping

- A broad evidence review rated practice testing as high utility across learning conditions: `src-learning-dunlosky-2013` (`systematic_review`).
- Classroom retrieval practice showed a positive aggregate effect with moderators and heterogeneity: `src-retrieval-meta-2021` (`meta_analysis`).

## Relevant signals

Study consists mainly of rereading or highlighting; material feels familiar but
cannot be produced without cues; or errors remain uncorrected after practice.

## Alternative explanations

Poor retrieval may reflect inadequate initial comprehension, ambiguous prompts,
missing prerequisite knowledge or a mismatch between practice and the required
application format.

## Information needed

The learning objective, material, prior understanding, expected response format,
availability and timing of correct feedback, delay until use and recurring
errors.

## Practical implications

Select prompts that require the form of knowledge needed later. Attempt an
answer before checking, correct it promptly and revisit errors rather than
counting exposure alone.

## Limitations

Effect size varies across learners, materials and settings. Retrieval practice
is not a complete curriculum and does not guarantee transfer to a substantially
different task.
