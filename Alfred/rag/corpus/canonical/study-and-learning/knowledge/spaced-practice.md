# Spaced Practice

## Operational definition

Spaced practice distributes contact with and retrieval of material across more
than one session separated in time. The interval allows some forgetting while
retaining a realistic chance of successful reconstruction and correction.

## What this concept does not mean

It is not postponing first contact, applying a universal interval formula or
splitting the same total time without later retrieval. A schedule alone does not
show that learning was retained.

## Main mechanisms

Returning after a delay requires reconstruction rather than continuous
familiarity. Performance after different intervals provides information for
adjusting subsequent spacing by item difficulty and the required retention
horizon.

## Evidence summary

Dunlosky and colleagues rated distributed practice as high utility. A 2024
systematic review in health-professions education generally supported spaced
learning while reporting heterogeneous designs and outcomes. The optimal
interval depends on material, learner and how long knowledge must be retained.

## Relevant signals

Most study is concentrated immediately before use; initial performance is good
but later recall is poor; or a large volume is reviewed once without later
retrieval.

## Alternative explanations

Low later performance may reflect weak initial comprehension. Spacing material
that was not understood can repeat confusion rather than resolve it.

## Information needed

The date and form of later use, material units, current understanding, available
opportunities, retrieval difficulty after intervals and total time constraints.

## Practical implications

Schedule at least two later retrieval opportunities when the horizon permits.
Shorten intervals for items that cannot yet be reconstructed and lengthen them
when retrieval is consistently successful.

## Limitations

The recent review is concentrated in health-professions education. No single
interval is optimal across materials, learners or retention horizons.
