# Goal Review

## Operational definition

Goal review compares the stated objective, behavior performed, progress,
current cost and changed conditions in order to choose explicitly among keeping,
replanning, rescheduling, replacing, pausing or abandoning the goal.

## What this concept does not mean

Review is not automatic reduction whenever discomfort appears, preservation of
a goal at any cost, or treating a change in plan as abandonment of the desired
outcome.

## Main mechanisms

Separating outcome, controllable behavior, deadline, measurement and conditions
locates the mismatch. A person can execute the planned behavior without seeing
the expected progress, or make progress at a cost that changes the decision.

## Evidence summary

BCTTv1 distinguishes review of behavioral goals from review of outcome goals.
A meta-analysis by Epton and colleagues found a small positive average effect
of goal setting across behaviors, with meaningful heterogeneity and moderators.
This supports calibrated review, not a universal prescription.

## Evidence mapping

- BCTTv1 distinguishes review of behavioral and outcome goals in light of performance: `src-bcttv1-2013` (`canonical_framework`).
- Goal setting had a small positive average effect, with heterogeneity and moderators: `src-goal-setting-2017` (`meta_analysis`).

## Relevant signals

Deadlines are repeatedly moved without new information; execution is adequate
but progress is not; progress requires disproportionate cost; the goal was
externally imposed; or work, health, care duties or resources changed.

## Alternative explanations

Low apparent progress may result from a poor measure, an incompatible plan, a
short observation window, third-party dependencies or a change in priority.

## Information needed

The current objective, controllable behavior, planned versus performed action,
progress measure, cost, current importance, changed constraints and the
appropriate decision horizon.

## Practical implications

Classify the mismatch before changing the goal. Record which element changed
and what observation would justify the next review.

## Limitations

Average effects do not determine the best adjustment for one person. Values,
resources and obligations can justify decisions that do not maximize measured
performance.
