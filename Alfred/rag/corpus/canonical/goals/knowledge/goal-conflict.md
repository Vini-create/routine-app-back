# Conflict and Facilitation Between Goals

## Operational definition

Goal conflict exists when pursuing one valued goal hinders another through incompatible strategies or competition for finite resources. Goal facilitation exists when pursuit of one goal supports another through shared means or outcomes.

## What this concept does not mean

Having several goals is not automatically conflict. Conflict and facilitation are not necessarily opposite ends of one scale: the same pair can share resources in one context and compete in another.

## Main mechanisms

Resource conflict consumes the same time, money, attention or recovery capacity. Inherent conflict uses strategies whose successful execution undermines each other. Facilitation can arise from shared actions, environments or intermediate outcomes.

## Evidence summary

A meta-analysis of 54 samples found goal conflict associated on average with lower positive outcomes and greater distress, with results varying by measurement and sample. Three multimethod studies by Riediger and Freund supported distinguishing interference from facilitation and linked them differently to well-being and persistent pursuit. These associations do not establish that conflict caused an individual's distress or nonperformance.

## Evidence mapping

- Goal conflict showed average associations with well-being and distress: `src-goal-conflict-meta-2017` (`meta_analysis`).
- Interference and facilitation behaved as distinct goal relations: `src-goal-interference-2004` (`multimethod_primary_studies`).

## Relevant signals

Two actions require the same limited period or resource, success in one undermines the other, or switching between goals repeatedly prevents meaningful progress.

## Alternative explanations

The goals may coexist but be overplanned, vague or individually infeasible. Apparent conflict may be a scheduling problem.

## Information needed

Each valued goal, required behaviors, shared constraints, incompatible strategies, deadlines, minimum viable progress and possible shared actions.

## Practical implications

Classify the conflict before prioritizing. Options include redesigning shared means, separating contexts, sequencing goals, reducing one target or deliberately deprioritizing one after checking autonomy and consequences.

## Limitations

Measures of goal conflict vary and much evidence is correlational. The concept should not turn every difficult value choice into a technical optimization problem.
