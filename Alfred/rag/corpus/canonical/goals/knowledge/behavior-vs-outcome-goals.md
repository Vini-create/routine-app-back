# Behavior Goals and Outcome Goals

## Operational definition

An outcome goal specifies a result, such as a score or completed project. A behavior goal specifies an observable action the person can perform. A process goal is useful here only when it names behavior rather than an internal slogan.

## What this concept does not mean

Outcome goals are not inherently bad, and behavior goals do not guarantee outcomes. Translating a result into action should not erase why the result matters.

## Main mechanisms

Outcome goals orient direction and evaluation. Behavior goals identify controllable responses that can be scheduled, observed and revised. Keeping both levels prevents a desired result from being mistaken for an executable plan.

## Evidence summary

The BCT Taxonomy distinguishes goal setting for behavior from goal setting for an outcome, supporting precise intervention description rather than superiority of either type. A meta-analysis of randomized behavior-change studies found a small positive unique average effect of goal setting with substantial moderator dependence. Evidence therefore supports using the distinction to improve decision clarity, not promising that one goal format will produce success.

## Evidence mapping

- Behavior and outcome goal setting have distinct operational definitions: `src-bcttv1-2013` (`consensus_taxonomy`).
- Goal setting produced a small average effect across heterogeneous behavior-change trials: `src-goal-setting-2017` (`systematic_review_meta_analysis`).

## Relevant signals

The user names only a desired result, tracks an outcome with delayed or external determinants, or cannot say which repeated action would make progress more likely.

## Alternative explanations

The next action may already be known but blocked by resources, capacity, conflict or aversiveness.

## Information needed

Desired result, controllable actions, external dependencies, measurement delay and the shortest useful review period.

## Practical implications

Retain the outcome as direction, add one observable behavior that is plausibly related, and monitor both without claiming individual causality.

## Limitations

Terminology varies across fields. The evidence does not support converting every meaningful aspiration into a single metric or ignoring outcome quality.
