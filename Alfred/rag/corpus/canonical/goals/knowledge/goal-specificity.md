# Goal Specificity

## Operational definition

Goal specificity is the degree to which a target identifies a discriminable result or performance level. An action plan separately states the behavior, context and timing used to pursue that target.

## What this concept does not mean

Specificity is not the same as difficulty, personal importance, measurability of every private experience or a complete execution plan. A precise target can still be impossible, imposed or strategically wrong.

## Main mechanisms

A specific target can direct attention, effort and evaluation toward a defined discrepancy. It also permits feedback to be interpreted against a reference. Planning then translates that reference into situated action.

## Evidence summary

Locke and Latham's review of goal-setting research describes specificity and difficulty as core goal properties whose effects depend on commitment, feedback, ability and task complexity. The BCT Taxonomy distinguishes goal setting from action planning and behavioral observation. Together, these sources support keeping the target and the execution plan conceptually distinct; they do not establish one universally effective specification format.

## Evidence mapping

- Specific goals can direct performance through attention, effort, persistence and strategy under important moderators: `src-goal-theory-2002` (`narrative_theoretical_review`).
- Goal setting and action planning are separately defined intervention components: `src-bcttv1-2013` (`consensus_taxonomy`).

## Relevant signals

Terms such as “improve,” “be consistent” or “get organized” have no defined target, observable indicator or boundary.

## Alternative explanations

The target may be clear while the first action, opportunity, resources or priority remain unclear.

## Information needed

Desired change, target level or boundary, observable indicator, timeframe appropriate to the behavior and who controls the outcome.

## Practical implications

Specify the target only as far as needed for a real decision, then create an action plan. For exploratory work, a bounded learning goal may be more appropriate than a premature performance target.

## Limitations

Classic evidence is concentrated in performance settings and cannot justify a universal “SMART” formula. Excess specificity can narrow attention or imply false precision.
