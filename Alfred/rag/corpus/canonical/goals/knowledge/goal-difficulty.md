# Goal Difficulty and Feasibility

## Operational definition

Goal difficulty is the level of performance or change required relative to current capability and conditions. Feasibility asks whether the target can plausibly be pursued with available time, skill, resources, feedback and dependencies.

## What this concept does not mean

More difficult is not always better, and feasibility is not an invitation to eliminate challenge. A missed target alone does not reveal whether difficulty, execution, measurement or context was responsible.

## Main mechanisms

Challenging specific goals can focus effort and persistence when commitment, ability, task knowledge and feedback are adequate. As complexity or uncertainty rises, learning, strategy development and staged progression may matter more than a fixed performance demand.

## Evidence summary

Locke and Latham's review describes a positive difficulty-performance relation under important moderators and boundary conditions. Epton and colleagues found that difficult goals moderated stronger average effects in randomized behavior-change studies, but the overall goal-setting effect was small and studies were heterogeneous. Neither source supplies an individually optimal difficulty level.

## Evidence mapping

- Ability, commitment, feedback and complexity moderate goal effects: `src-goal-theory-2002` (`narrative_theoretical_review`).
- Difficult goals were associated with larger average effects in one behavior-change meta-analysis: `src-goal-setting-2017` (`systematic_review_meta_analysis`).

## Relevant signals

The required pace exceeds demonstrated performance, depends on unavailable resources, combines several untested increases or leaves no allowance for known constraints.

## Alternative explanations

The goal may be feasible but vague, poorly scheduled, externally imposed or competing with another goal.

## Information needed

Baseline performance, target and deadline, skill, resources, dependencies, recent cost, uncertainty and acceptable minimum.

## Practical implications

Identify which constraint makes the current target implausible. Adjust one of scope, pace, deadline, support or progression, then define a review point using observed performance and cost.

## Limitations

Much classic evidence concerns assigned performance tasks. This concept does not set clinical, educational or exercise prescriptions and should preserve domain-specific safety rules.
