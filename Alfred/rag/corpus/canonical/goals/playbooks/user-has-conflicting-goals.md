# User Has Conflicting Goals

## Activation criteria

The user names at least two valued goals and pursuing one appears to hinder the other through shared finite resources or incompatible strategies.

## Similar situations that should not activate this playbook

Do not activate merely because a schedule is full. Use too-many-habits routing for many simultaneous new routines, or insufficient-capacity routing when one plan exceeds current resources without a second valued goal.

## Possible explanations

The goals may compete for time, money, attention or recovery; require mutually undermining actions; have deadlines that overlap; or only appear incompatible because their behaviors and minimum viable progress are undefined.

## Missing information

The goals in the user's own terms, why each matters, required behaviors, shared constraints, deadlines, minimum acceptable progress, consequences of delay and any action that could facilitate both.

## Response objective

Name the type of conflict and support an explicit choice among coexistence, redesign, sequencing or deliberate deprioritization. Preserve autonomy and avoid silently selecting the goal that seems more productive.

## Decision path

- If goals share an action or enabling resource, redesign for facilitation before cutting either target.
- If the conflict is scheduling, protect minimum viable opportunities and separate contexts.
- If a fixed deadline creates temporary competition, sequence the goals and set a review date.
- If strategies are inherently incompatible, ask which value and consequence governs the current horizon.
- If one goal is externally imposed, examine endorsement and nonnegotiable obligations separately.

## Candidate strategies

Shared-means design, minimum viable coexistence, temporal sequencing, scope revision, explicit priority rule or deliberate pause.

## Conditions for selecting each strategy

Coexistence must fit actual resources; sequencing needs a restart condition; deprioritization must acknowledge consequences. Safety and legal obligations constrain choice.

## When to ask a question

Ask which resource or strategy makes the goals interfere when the conflict is asserted but not observable.

## When to respond directly

Respond directly when the conflict type, deadlines and user preference are already clear.

## What to avoid

Do not use a generic priority matrix without the user's values, promise perfect balance, or treat goal conflict as evidence of poor organization.

## Suggested next step

Write the two goals, the exact interference and the selected resolution for the current review horizon, including when that choice will be reconsidered.
